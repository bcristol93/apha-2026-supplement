Figure 3 Interpretation:
(3A) Spatial Mobility Network Archetypes: Conceptual taxonomy categorizing metropolitan mobility structures into three archetypes: Type I (Transit-Radial Multi-Hub), where transit-corridor mixing creates cross-CBG exposure uncaptured by M1; Type II (Hub-and-Spoke Mixed Density), where core mixing and suburban return flows drive asymmetric exposure modeled explicitly by M2; and Type III (Car-Dependent Dispersed), where uniform vehicle flows cause M2's spatial force-of-infection to collapse toward M1's homogeneous mixing assumption.

(3B) Weekly $R_t$ Trajectories Across Archetypes (2022–2026): Comparison of weekly reproduction numbers ($R_t$) between M1 (solid) and M2 (dashed) with 95% ensemble credible bands across four post-COVID seasons in four geographically diverse metros (Chicago, Las Vegas, Nashville, Lubbock). Model divergence peaks in transit-heavy (Type I) and centralized commuter (Type II) networks, but converges in dispersed car-dependent systems (Type III).
