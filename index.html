📌 Poster Note: This webpage contains updated high-resolution figures and revised model comparative analysis (M1 vs. M2) supplementing the physical poster presented at APHA 2026.
Figure 3A: Peak Rₜ Disparity by Metro-Season (M1 vs. M2)
Figure 3A Interpretation: Each row presents a solid left bar (M1) and shaded right bar (M2) representing peak Rₜ for a metro-season. Metros are separated according to spatial network archetype (Type I–III) and ordered within categories from largest to smallest peak Rₜ disparity (M1 − M2), indicating greater spatial redistribution in M2. An asterisk (*) denotes metro-season outliers in which M2 > M1 (12/100).
Figure 3B: Mean Absolute Error Across Epidemic Features
Figure 3B Interpretation: Violin plots show the distribution of mean absolute error (MAE) of M1 vs. M2 across 4 epidemic features: duration, onset week, peak week, and peak intensity. Each violin plot contains 100 uniquely colored points (metro-seasons) dispersed relative to feature medians. Paired errors (M1 vs. M2) compared via Wilcoxon signed-rank test showed duration, peak week, and peak intensity were statistically significant (p < 0.005), with M2 showing lower MAE for duration while M1 performed significantly better on peak week and peak intensity.
Figure 3C: WIS Retrospective Forecasting Performance
Figure 3C Interpretation: Weighted Interval Score (WIS) computed over the 2025–26 held-out forecasting window (MMWR weeks 21–34) and displayed on a log scale. Lower scores indicate more precise forecasts. Each row connects M1 (filled) and M2 (open) points, with green segments indicating metros where M2 outperformed M1 (18/25) and red segments for the inverse. Note: NYC, Maricopa (Phoenix), and St. Louis were obtained from ESSENCE/EpiQuery with shorter held-out windows.
